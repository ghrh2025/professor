The Five Concepts You'll Choose From

Vertical Channel Conflict — disagreement between different levels of the channel (e.g., manufacturer vs. retailer)
Horizontal Channel Conflict — disagreement between members at the same channel level
Coercive Channel Power — one member forces compliance by threatening penalties or withholding access
Contractual Control — using formal franchise agreements or exclusive contracts to bind and coordinate channel partners
Direct Channel Disruption — a company bypasses traditional intermediaries to control the path to the customer

Debrief: The Concepts Behind Each Company

Apple — Coercive Channel Power

The App Store is the only way to reach iPhone users. No alternative path exists. Apple forces compliance by threatening removal — that's coercive power. The 30% fee exists because developers have no other option.

Nike — Horizontal Channel Conflict

Two retailers at the same level of Nike's channel competing for the same customers. Foot Locker's clearance sale hurts the local shop — not because Nike told them to, but because Nike authorized too many retailers in the same area. The conflict is retailer vs. retailer, not Nike vs. retailer.

Subway — Vertical Channel Conflict + Contractual Control

Corporate sets the price. Franchisees pay the cost. Two different levels of the channel with opposite incentives — that's vertical conflict. The franchise contract gives Subway the legal right to enforce it — that's contractual control.

John Deere — Vertical Channel Conflict + Coercive Power

The software lock routes all repair revenue through authorized dealers — extending channel control past the point of sale. Farmers with broken equipment during harvest have zero negotiating power. That's coercive power operating through product design.
