What a Car Really Costs

Keep this. You will use it before you use anything else from this course.

The Point

The payment was never the price.

A dealership will happily talk to you about one number for two hours: the monthly payment. It is the number they can move without giving anything up. They lengthen the loan, the payment drops, and you pay thousands more.

Everything on this card exists to keep you looking at the other numbers.

Step 1

Build the real monthly number

Five costs, not one. Fill in what you expect. The insurance line is the one that surprises people under 25, and it is the only one you can find out for free in four minutes.

That last line is not optional. Something breaks. Budgeting zero for it is how a $300 car becomes a crisis.

Step 2

Price the insurance before you fall in love with the car

Insurance is priced on the car, not just on you. A coupe and a sedan of the same value can be hundreds apart a year. Get the quote while you can still change your mind.

Go straight to the carriers. geico.com and progressive.com both quote in a few minutes with a VIN or just a year, make and model.

Skip the comparison sites. They are lead-generation businesses. The quote is the bait and your phone number is the product. You will be called for weeks. That is not a scam, it is a business model, and noticing it is the marketing lesson in this card.

Step 3

See what the loan length actually costs

Same car, same rate. Only the number of months changes.

TermMonthlyTotal paidInterest

The longest term always has the friendliest monthly payment and the ugliest total. It also keeps you upside down — owing more than the car is worth — for years, which means you cannot sell it or trade it without writing a check.

Step 4

The four numbers that decide everything

  • Out-the-door price. The single number including tax, title, doc fee, everything. Not the sticker. Not the monthly.
  • APR. Get pre-approved at a credit union first so you have a rate to beat. Dealer financing is a product they sell at a markup.
  • Term in months. Shortest payment you can genuinely afford. 48 or 60, not 84.
  • Total of payments. Monthly times term. This is the real price of the car. Say it out loud before you sign.

Step 5

What to say

When they ask your monthly budgetI'd rather not start there. What's the out-the-door price on this car?
When the number keeps movingCan you email me the buyer's order with everything itemized? I'll look at it tonight.
When there's a fee you don't recognizeWhat is that line, and is it optional?
When they need an answer todayThen it's a no today. I'm fine either way.

You are allowed to leave. Nothing in a dealership expires. The person who can walk out gets the better price, every time, and everyone in the building knows it.

Walk away when

  • They will only discuss monthly payment and will not put an out-the-door price in writing.
  • The price changes once you are in the finance office.
  • You are told the deal is only good today.
  • A used car has no inspection allowed and no history report offered.
  • You feel rushed. That feeling is a product too.

One more thing

New cars lose the most money in the quietest way

Depreciation is usually the single largest cost of owning a new car, and it never arrives as a bill you pay. A two or three year old car has already taken that hit for you, which is most of the argument for buying used.
