Week 5.2b · How the midterm asks it
Apply it. Don't define it.
The midterm won't ask you what segmentation means. It will hand you a company and ask you to make the call. Three practice rounds, each harder than the last.
1SegmentPut the customers into groups that behave differently. Never by income.
2TargetPick one group, and say out loud who you are choosing to lose.
3Prove itTwo facts from the prompt. A strong answer quotes the evidence. A weak one gives an opinion.
Round 1 of 3 · Small and local
Spoke & Gravel, Loveland

Spoke & Gravel is a 900-square-foot bike shop two blocks from the Little Miami Scenic Trail. It has two mechanics and one repair stand each. Three kinds of customers walk in. From May through September, trail riders rent hybrids by the hour and ask where the ice cream place is. Year-round, road cyclists come in once or twice a year for a professional fitting and talk about $4,000 bikes. Most of them say they'll "think about it" and order online. Every week of the year, food-delivery riders from the apartments on Route 48 bring in worn brake pads and flat tires, and they need the bike back the same day because no bike means no pay that night.

Last year the delivery riders used 58% of mechanic hours and never missed paying a bill. Road cyclists accounted for 4 bike sales. Rentals brought in money for five months and nothing for the other seven. The owner wants to put "Same-Day Repair, Every Day" on the window. Her business partner wants a glass case of carbon road bikes, because "that's where the money is."

Which group should Spoke & Gravel target?
What did you split them on (not income), who is the shop choosing to lose, and which two facts from the prompt prove your pick?

Round 2 of 3 · Mid-sized
The Mill Creek Mudcats

The Mudcats are a minor-league baseball team in a 6,000-seat ballpark off I-75. Last season they finished in last place and still sold more tickets than the year before. Here are five people who came to a game.

Tom has held season tickets behind home plate since 2004 and keeps score by hand. Doug drives 90 minutes from Dayton and knows every player's stats. The Reyes family comes for Friday fireworks with two young kids and leaves in the sixth inning. Kayla came with eleven coworkers on a company outing and spent the whole game on the party deck without asking the score. Priya books the party deck twice a season for her office and has already asked about dates for next year.

The party deck has sold out every date for three seasons, and the team turned away about 40 group bookings last year. The team has money for one project: better seats behind home plate, a second party deck, or a kids' play area.

Sort the five into three groups based on why each person comes to the ballpark. Give each group a name.

Which project should the Mudcats build?

Round 3 of 3 · National
Anchor Fitness

Anchor Fitness runs 900 gyms across the country and charges $15 a month. Its members fall into two groups based on how often they actually show up.

Most members (about 7 in 10) are casual. They joined in January, come in once every couple of weeks, walk on a treadmill, and leave. They rarely cancel, and many say they picked Anchor because "nobody's watching you." About 1 in 7 members are regulars. They come five days a week, lift heavy, and fill every squat rack between 5 and 8 p.m. They post online about the wait for racks and mention that the gym across the street has twice as many.

Each Anchor gym can hold about 300 people at once. Headquarters has money for one change in every gym: swap half the treadmills and machines for 20 new squat racks, or add more beginner machines and a quiet, mirror-free workout room.

Which group should Anchor target?
What does Anchor give up by choosing them, and which two facts from the prompt prove your pick?
