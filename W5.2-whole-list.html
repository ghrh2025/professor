Week 5.2 · Segmentation and Targeting
Segment, then target
Two different jobs, and Chapter 9 does them in that order. Segmenting is cutting the market into groups. Targeting is choosing which group you are for, which means choosing who you are willing to lose.
1SegmentCut the market into groups that behave differently from each other.
2TargetPick the group you are built for, and accept that the others will walk.
Aldi built four aisles for somebody. Your job is to describe her without using how much money she makes. The store did not, and that is why one in three American households shopped there last year.

Week 5.2 · Phase 2
Three shoppers. Who is Aldi for?
Targeting is a choice about who you are willing to lose. Make the call on all three, then defend the one that costs you something.
Steph Colvin
Quarter in the cart, four aisles, own bags, out in twenty minutes. Comes in under the number she had in her head and likes saying so.
Angela Boyd
Wanted the coffee she drinks and half a pound of turkey sliced thin. Got to the register, realised she had to bag her own groceries into bags she had not brought, and left with nothing. Drove to the Kroger.
Dev Raman
Does the big trip at Aldi every week, then drives to Kroger for the coffee, the deli counter and the one brand of cereal his kids will eat.
Nobody stopped Angela Boyd. Nobody offered to order her coffee. Why was letting her drive to Kroger worth it?

Week 5.2 · Phase 3
Three stores, three plays
All three sell groceries within a few miles of each other. None of them is running the same targeting strategy. The case never labels any of them.
From the case Walmart is not built to be right for one kind of shopper. Kroger stacks its own brands in three tiers so three different shoppers each find a version priced the way they think groceries ought to be. Aldi carries about 1,800 items and refuses to carry anything else.
