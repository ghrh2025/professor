Week 2.2 · Grids Are Not the Point
A grid is a parking lot
Nobody gets paid for sorting facts into boxes. The grid exists so you can cross its boxes and drive a decision out of it. Two crossings matter:
Strength × Opportunity
The Best Move
Spend a strength you own on an opening that exists. This is where strategy comes from.
Weakness × Threat
The Vulnerability
Where an outside force hits you exactly where you're already weak. This is what keeps CEOs up at night.
Two rounds today. Round 1: build Nike's best move and worst nightmare from Monday's grid, then your group kills the weakest move on the table. Round 2: a brand-new company, no card bank, no help — you build the grid AND the move from scratch. That's the skill the midterm tests.

Week 2.2 · Round 2 — New Client Briefing (1 of 2)
Meet Crumbl
You've never analyzed this company. Read fast — the facts you need are all here.
In 2017, cousins Jason McGowan and Sawyer Hemsley opened a cookie shop in Logan, Utah, with one chocolate chip recipe they'd perfected by A/B-testing batches on customers. What made Crumbl famous wasn't the cookie — it was the calendar: every Sunday night, the menu rotates, a handful of new oversized flavors drop, and last week's disappear. The weekly reveal turned into a social media ritual — review videos of each week's drop rack up millions of views, and the oversized cookies in the pink box became their own content format. Crumbl grew faster than almost any food brand in American history, and it did it with almost no traditional advertising.
Week 2.2 · Round 2 — New Client Briefing (2 of 2)
Crumbl by the numbers
Same rule as Monday: these are facts, not conclusions. Where each one belongs is your call.
1,101 stores
Net 42 new locations added last year, with 99 more openings planned and 206 signed franchise agreements waiting.
−15.6%
Average sales per store fell to $1.14 million last year — the chain keeps opening stores while each store sells less.
100% franchised
Every store is owned by a franchisee who paid roughly $850K–$1.5M to open it. Crumbl runs zero stores itself.
One product
Dessert — mostly cookies. Copycat cookie chains have multiplied, and grocery stores now sell oversized cookies in lookalike boxes.
Hint from Monday: a falling number is an outcome. Sort what's driving it.
